# COLD OPEN

INT. SMITH GARAGE - MORNING

A toaster-sized machine sits on Rick's bench. Its label reads RETURN POLICY. A smaller label reads POLICY SUBJECT TO REALITY.

Six windows hover above it. Only the first is lit. Rick places a mug inside the machine and hits it with a hammer.

MORTY
That was Mom's mug.

RICK
It was an extremely local arrangement of mug. Watch the warranty.

He turns a dial. The windows overlap. A pristine mug appears beside the fragments.

MORTY
You fixed it?

RICK
I broadened where we shop for the word fixed.

Morty reaches for the replacement. It has a different child's handprint on it.

MORTY
That says "Love, Brundle."

RICK
Unrelated manufacturing detail.

The original fragments remain. Rick covers them with a towel bearing the words LABORATORY PROCEDURE.

MORTY
I thought the whole point of a return was getting back to before.

RICK
The whole point of a return is making someone else operate the desk.

On Rick's monitor: WORLD WEIGHTS [1, 0, 0, 0, 0, 0]. Below it, FIELD ENERGY: 0.

He gestures toward a green aperture forming behind the bench.

RICK
Three connected demonstration worlds. Nothing crosses the perimeter. A beautifully constrained bad idea.

Jerry enters, sees the intact mug, and picks up the machine's promotional brochure.

JERRY
Can this do a Tuesday?

Rick and Morty look at him.

JERRY
Not this Tuesday. I don't want to get greedy.

EXT. THREE-WORLD DEMONSTRATION PLAZA - CONTINUOUS

Rick and Morty step into a showroom with three enormous doors. Each has a different sky behind it. Beyond the showroom, a dark railing surrounds three more doors.

A host with a smiling rectangular face rolls forward. A badge reads MANAGER. Its smile is printed on a removable panel.

MANAGER
Welcome. If you are dissatisfied with your consequences, retain your receipt.

MORTY
That's a weird thing to say before anything happens.

RICK
That's retail, Morty. Threats with carpeting.

A floor map shows light spreading among the first three windows. The other three remain dark. Rick points to a small moving graph.

RICK
Ensemble weights. The display is not telling you which door your particular shoes are going to choose.

Morty nods. He has already put one shoe through a door.

From far away: a small CRACK. A decorative fountain drops half an inch. Rick glances at FIELD ENERGY: 0.0982, then angles the monitor away.

MORTY
What was that?

RICK
The floor acknowledging a feature.

At home, Jerry tears the customer copy off the brochure. It says STORE CREDIT ONLY.

JERRY
Honestly? Progress.

The plaza's manager rotates its smile upside down.

# ACT ONE

INT. DEMONSTRATION PLAZA - RECEIPT COUNTER

The manager offers Morty a little paper crown: SATISFIED VISITOR.

MORTY
I haven't decided if I'm satisfied.

MANAGER
The crown is provisional. Removing it requires a different crown.

Morty bumps a glass ornament off the counter. It breaks. A worker begins gathering the pieces.

MORTY
Sorry. I can help.

Rick produces another ornament from a neighboring doorway.

RICK
Or we can help faster by being done.

The worker keeps sweeping the original pieces. Morty notices.

The manager scans his wrist. On the monitor a register lights up: RECORD P(1): 0.399466611.

MORTY
Does that mean I'm thirty-nine percent guilty?

RICK
No. It means a controlled gate changed a bit in one world sector. Guilt is the free accessory your brain keeps installing.

MANAGER
Our transit register permits us to recognize your visit after a relocation.

MORTY
So there's a receipt for the receipt?

MANAGER
We call that trust.

The worker sweeps around their feet. Morty kneels and picks up a large shard.

RICK
Careful. That's from the world where it was already broken.

MORTY
I watched myself break it.

Rick holds out the perfect replacement.

RICK
And now you're watching me provide an attractive second fact.

Morty sets the shard down beside the new ornament. The two facts do not cancel.

INT. SMITH KITCHEN - SAME TIME

Jerry has connected the Return Policy's customer terminal to the kitchen television. A friendly menu asks WHAT WOULD YOU LIKE TO REPLACE?

JERRY
I said something at a neighborhood meeting.

SUMMER
That could be any meeting you've ever attended.

JERRY
I called the speed bump a "road ottoman." People thought I was making a point.

Summer records this on a notepad labeled THINGS DAD WANTS UNHAPPENED.

Beth examines the mug with the alien child's handprint.

BETH
Who's Brundle?

JERRY
Possibly a loyalty program.

The television generates an apology certificate: THIS HOUSEHOLD HAS ELECTED TO CONSIDER THE MATTER RESOLVED.

Jerry relaxes. His phone rings. NEIGHBORHOOD GROUP.

JERRY
Why can they still call?

SUMMER
Because you printed a sentence.

The mug rattles. A thin ripple travels across the kitchen tiles. Summer puts her notepad on top of it as though paper might hold the floor down.

On the television: FIELD ENERGY 0.2548. RECORDED BIT PROBABILITY 0.399466611.

BETH
Does your father know this thing is moving the grout?

SUMMER
The grandpa father or the certificate father?

Jerry peers at the apology certificate.

JERRY
I think I bought the version that only apologizes to me.

INT. DEMONSTRATION PLAZA - BOUNDARY CONTROL

The plaza has developed a gentle slope. The manager slides downhill with professional composure.

MANAGER
Your displacement is producing a local field response. We recommend expanding your service area.

Rick examines the return cable. A green waveform feeds into the energy settings of the doors, which feed back into the waveform.

MORTY
Is the machine making the place react, then reacting to what it made?

RICK
That is a very accusatory description of a feedback system.

Rick finds the switch labeled BOUNDARY LINKS. It is locked.

MORTY
You said it was constrained.

RICK
And now the constraint is constraining my options. Development is a journey.

He opens the switch cover.

The manager offers an EXTENDED CONSEQUENCE PLAN. Morty pushes the brochure away.

MORTY
Maybe turn the machine off first?

RICK
We have three empty worlds behind that boundary. We spread the load, everyone gets a smaller problem.

MORTY
Did you ask the empty worlds if they're empty?

Rick flicks the switch. The three dark doors gain green edges.

For one quiet instant the exterior probability display still reads 0.000000.

RICK
See? Very respectful rollout.

One second later, light begins crawling through the newly opened links. Behind a door, a CIVIC MEETING is interrupted by their ceiling becoming a showroom floor.

CHAIRPERSON (O.S.)
We will add this to new business.

# ACT TWO

EXT. WORLD FIVE - CIVIC SQUARE

Rick and Morty stumble into a square full of municipal folding chairs. A brass statue depicts somebody filling out a form.

The chairperson, a many-jointed woman named VENN, holds a microphone connected to nothing.

VENN
Are you the expansion?

RICK
Depends on whether that's a compliment or a zoning category.

Venn points to a fissure running through the square. Green light pulses beneath it. Rick's handheld shows EXTERIOR WEIGHT: 0.258753.

MORTY
That's a quarter of the weight outside already.

RICK
Probability weight. Please don't turn the instrument into a population census.

VENN
Whatever unit you've chosen, my meeting is leaning.

Morty sees a child carefully lifting chairs away from the fissure. He starts helping.

Rick scans the sky. The waveform is now etched across it like a giant return address.

MANAGER
(emerging from the aperture)
Good news. Your plan now includes these customers.

VENN
We didn't purchase anything.

MANAGER
That was the good news.

Venn turns off her unconnected microphone. The lights in the square dim anyway.

MORTY
We have to stay and fix this.

Rick looks from the damaged square to the still-functioning doors.

RICK
We have to stay in the general category of fixing this. Geography is negotiable.

He lifts a device with two world numbers on it and presses SWAP.

INT. REPLACEMENT SHOWROOM - CONTINUOUS

The plaza changes around them. Broken tiles become immaculate. The ceiling rises. A fountain runs correctly for the first time.

Rick spreads his arms.

RICK
Location problem solved. That was most of the visible problem.

Morty checks his wrist. The little transit mark remains.

At the desk, the same manager pulls a receipt from a slot.

MANAGER
Welcome back.

RICK
We have never been to this desk.

MANAGER
The desk agrees. Your record doesn't.

On the scanner: WORLD SWAP 1 / 4. RECORD P(1): 0.399466611.

MORTY
It came with us.

RICK
The world labels were permuted. The record register wasn't. That's correct operation presented in a very hostile font.

Through the new showroom window, the sky still carries the broad green waveform.

The manager rotates its faceplate to a neutral expression.

MANAGER
Replacement premises do not include replacement history.

Morty sets the shard from the first ornament on the desk. He has been carrying it in his pocket.

MORTY
Neither does replacing the ornament.

For a moment Rick has no immediate line. He takes out a second device instead.

RICK
Okay. New approach. We make the machine less interested in which world agrees with which other world.

The manager quietly puts a bucket under a brand-new crack.

INT. SMITH KITCHEN / SHOWROOM - INTERCUT

Summer has covered the kitchen table with handwritten cards: CRACK IN FLOOR. MUG BELONGS TO BRUNDLE. DAD STILL IN GROUP CHAT.

Beth is on speakerphone with Morty.

BETH
Why is the grout making that noise?

MORTY
It's a field thing. We're working on it.

SUMMER
Is the field thing why my phone says the kitchen is under warranty in six places?

Rick pulls the phone toward himself.

RICK
Do not accept any cookies from the floor.

Jerry enters with a second certificate.

JERRY
I found "erase associated record." That's what we want, right?

Summer takes the certificate from him and writes PAPER STILL EXISTS on another card.

JERRY
That's not a very encouraging filing system.

SUMMER
It's not an encouragement system.

At the showroom, Venn appears in the doorway carrying a chair with one short leg. She sets it beside Rick.

VENN
Your new location has excellent parking. I followed the damage.

RICK
That is aggressively inconvenient investigative work.

Venn sits. The chair rocks. She folds a promotional brochure and puts it under the short leg.

VENN
There. One of your documents has solved something.

Morty laughs despite himself. Rick stares at the brochure shim longer than he needs to.

INT. REPLACEMENT SHOWROOM - PHASE CONTROL BOOTH

Rick assembles a helmet out of display headphones and a blender lid. He puts it on the machine.

MORTY
Why does the machine need headphones?

RICK
It doesn't. I need the salespeople to stop asking what I'm doing.

He raises DEPHASING to 0.65. Behind the display, the crisp intersections between glowing world windows begin to soften.

The windows remain.

MORTY
Are you deleting the other worlds?

RICK
No. Losing phase relationships. The probability is still there. Worlds do not get deleted because I bought the noise setting.

MANAGER
Noise is included in all plans.

Rick sets a mug on the floor. Its jitter lessens. Across the showroom, the broad sag in the floor remains.

MORTY
It looks quieter.

RICK
Quiet is a diagnostic category with a great publicist.

On the monitor, coherence begins at 0.534673. The record indicator holds at 0.399466611.

Venn watches a crack creep under her chair.

VENN
Does quieter usually mean the crack stops?

Rick puts one finger over the field-energy readout, fails to cover it completely, and removes his hand.

RICK
Not automatically.

The manager offers Venn headphones. She uses them to tie the two broken pieces of the chair leg together.

INT. REPLACEMENT SHOWROOM - LATER

The overlapping lights have settled into a subdued haze. Coherence reads 0.290300. Field energy reads 0.525872, higher than before.

The manager plays a tranquil customer-satisfaction melody. It is slightly out of tune because the speaker is bending.

MORTY
The interference went down. The field energy went up.

RICK
Different quantities. That's why I put different labels on them. A rare triumph for labels today.

VENN
Your machine is still feeding the thing that is bending my square.

Rick examines the cable loop again. Source into field. Field into world energies. Worlds into source.

MORTY
You can break the loop.

RICK
I can. Then the showroom stops being a showroom. All those conveniently available next destinations stop being conveniently available.

Morty looks at the pristine ornament on the counter. Beside it: the shard, the chair, and a slowly filling bucket.

MORTY
So the machine works as long as we keep deciding the mess is somewhere else.

RICK
The equations do not contain that sentence.

MORTY
I know. I'm talking about us.

Rick's hand hovers over the power isolation controls.

Before he presses them, the manager presents a large red button.

MANAGER
There is one further resolution option. Clear associated record.

The button is much larger and easier to reach.

# ACT THREE

The transit light goes out. On the monitor, RECORD P(1) becomes 0.000000.

The field-energy display stays near 0.548433. The floor does not rise. The bucket keeps filling.

Venn holds up her chair. Summer, still on the phone, holds up her paper cards. Beth holds the Brundle mug. Morty holds the shard.

MORTY
Did it erase what happened to us?

Rick looks at the room instead of his scanner.

RICK
This is one bit. You are extremely more annoying storage than one bit.

MANAGER

VENN

INT. SMITH KITCHEN / SHOWROOM - INTERCUT

Jerry watches the apology certificate vanish from his television account. His phone lights up with the neighborhood chat again.

INT. REPLACEMENT SHOWROOM - MAIN CONTROLS

Rick opens three real switches: SOURCE, RETURN COUPLING, PORTAL COUPLING.

RICK
The source stops driving the field. The field stops shifting the transport energies. The transport links stop moving weight between worlds.

MORTY
And everything goes back?

Rick checks the populations. Six nonzero columns. Almost 0.478337 of the weight is in the exterior group.

RICK
No. Those are the settings for stopping what we're doing now. They aren't a rewind button.

He looks up at Morty.

RICK
I should have led with that.

Morty doesn't give him a speech or let him escape into a joke. He nods once.

The three switches go dark. The showroom doors stop cycling. The green field across the sky remains, but its faster ripples begin to settle.

Venn checks her square through the ordinary service entrance. Still bent. No longer adding a new bend every time a customer moves.

MANAGER
How do I classify the outcome?

RICK
Interrupted ongoing cause. Incomplete repair. Put it in a box large enough to be useful.

The manager tears RETURN POLICY off the counter and writes REPAIR DESK on the back.

Morty goes to help move the first broken tile.

Rick takes the other end. For once, they both lift the same object in the same direction.

EXT. CIVIC SQUARE - AFTER THE SHUTDOWN

A modest work crew repairs the square. The sky is still visibly distorted.

Rick checks the monitor: the world weights have stopped changing. The record is zero. Field energy has fallen to 0.389351.

MORTY
It's coming down.

RICK
The energy is. Look at the other number.

The maximum field amplitude has crept above two.

MORTY
That went up.

RICK
Damping takes energy out of motion. A field can still sit at an offset. Flat isn't the same setting as never touched.

Venn sets a spirit level on a repaired bench. It is not perfect. She slides a small shim under one leg.

VENN
Then we'll work with where it is.

Rick watches, pocketing the handheld without trying to improve the sentence.

The manager arrives carrying the original broken ornament. Morty and the worker glue it back together. The seam shows.

MORTY
Sorry I broke this.

WORKER
Thank you for saying which one.

They return home through a separate emergency extraction device. Rick labels it EXTERNAL EQUIPMENT before Morty can ask.

MORTY
You could've gotten us home that way the whole time?

RICK
Getting home wasn't the hard part.

He looks back at the work crew, then closes the device.

BRUNDLE

BETH
You're Brundle.

BRUNDLE
